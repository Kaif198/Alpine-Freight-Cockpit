# 60-second interview walkthrough

**0–12 seconds — the decision.** “A European freight tender is a trade-off between price, service and available capacity. I built Alpine Freight Cockpit to bring those decisions into one clear view. The market sources are real; the tender partners and performance are explicitly fictional.”

**12–28 seconds — inspect a bid.** Open Tender Benchmark, search Hamburg, and select Summit Meridian's premium bid. “Here I can see the price above our reference and inspect the exact calculation. The reference combines observed diesel with synthetic bid consensus. I show that limitation rather than presenting it as a market quote.”

**28–45 seconds — make the trade-off.** Open Volume Allocation and choose Hamburg. Move the reliability slider from cost-first to service-first. “The plan changes while respecting each partner's capacity and our concentration limit. I can see the cost, partner mix and binding constraints. If I request too much volume, the tool explains why the plan is infeasible.”

**45–60 seconds — explain the process.** “The static website reads committed JSON. A daily workflow refreshes approved publisher sources, validates the results and keeps the last good file if a source fails. Every real figure has provenance; every fictional result is labelled. The goal is an understandable, auditable decision, not just a dashboard.”

Before the interview, confirm the current source badges and hosted deployment status. Do not describe unavailable carrier surcharges as live or synthetic savings as actual Red Bull savings.
